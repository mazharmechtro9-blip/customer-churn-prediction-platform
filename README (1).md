# Enterprise Customer Retention & Revenue Protection Platform

An end-to-end customer churn intelligence platform built on the Telco Customer
Churn dataset (7,043 customers, 21 features). Goes beyond a simple churn
classifier to answer the business question: **which customers are likely to
churn, why, how much revenue is at risk, and what should we do about it?**

## Problem

Telecom providers lose recurring revenue every month to preventable churn.
This project identifies at-risk customers, quantifies the dollar value of
that risk, and recommends a specific retention action per customer —
turning a prediction into a decision.

## Key Findings

- Overall churn rate: **26.5%** of customers
- Month-to-month contracts churn at **42.7%**, versus just **2.8%** for
  two-year contracts — the single largest driver of churn
- Fiber customers with no tech-support add-on churn well above average
- Estimated monthly revenue at risk: **$203,032/mo** (~$2.4M annualized)
- Model catches **78.3%** of actual churners (Recall) with an **ROC-AUC
  of 84.4%** on the held-out test set

## Approach

1. Data cleaning & validation
2. EDA driven by business questions (not just charts for their own sake)
3. RFM-based customer segmentation (K-Means)
4. Churn classification (Logistic Regression baseline → Random Forest → XGBoost)
5. Class imbalance handling
6. Model explainability (SHAP) — why each customer is at risk, not just their score
7. Revenue-at-risk scoring (probability × customer value)
8. Rule-based retention action engine
9. Interactive Streamlit dashboard with single-customer and batch-CSV modes

## Model Performance

Evaluated on a held-out 20% test set (never seen during training):

| Metric | Score |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | 76.1% |
| Precision | 53.4% |
| Recall | 78.3% |
| F1 Score | 63.5% |
| ROC-AUC | 84.4% |

The model is deliberately tuned to favor **recall over precision** — in
churn prevention, missing a real churner (losing that revenue permanently)
costs more than occasionally offering a retention discount to a customer
who was never going to leave.


which are generated by running the modeling notebook in `notebooks/`.
